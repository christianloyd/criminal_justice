Sociological School

Sociological Theories

The dominant modern paradigm in criminology. It argues that crime is not born in the individual, but created by social structures, peer processes, and structural conflict.

Social Structure Theories

Crime is caused by the way society is organized. Poverty, inequality, blocked opportunities, and deteriorating neighborhoods generate crime regardless of the individuals living there.

Social Process Theories

Crime is learned through interactions with others. Anyone, regardless of class, can become a criminal if exposed to criminal definitions, or if their social bonds to society are broken.

Social Conflict Theories

Society is in constant conflict over resources and power. "Crime" is simply a label the powerful use to criminalize the behavior of the powerless and protect their own interests.
